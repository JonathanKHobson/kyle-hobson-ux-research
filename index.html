Keep the question and the decision connected.

Useful research makes its reasoning visible. Your team should be able to trace a recommendation back to what was observed, understand its limits, and decide what to do next.

  1. Frame the decision

    We clarify the product question, what you already know, and what evidence would help your team act.

  2. Gather and examine

    I plan and carry out the agreed research, keeping methods, observations and interpretation traceable.

  3. Make the next step clear

    We review the findings, their limits and the recommendations. You receive the agreed research materials and a practical handoff.

Before we talk

What kind of team is this for?

Product teams, founders and mission-driven organizations with a specific audience, usability or prioritization question. We can begin with a focused engagement or agree on a defined period of research support.

Can you help us choose a research method?

Yes. We start with what you need to decide and what access you have. An interview, survey, usability session, expert review or analysis of existing material answers different questions; the scope should make that distinction clear.

What about recruitment and participant information?

We agree on participant criteria, recruitment responsibilities, consent, incentives and information handling before research begins. Please describe your audience in the inquiry form without sharing participant records.

Will research prove our idea will succeed?

Research can expose risks, clarify needs and inform a decision. It cannot guarantee adoption or business results. I explain what the evidence supports and what would still need to be tested.

How are cost and timing decided?

I propose the deliverables, fee, schedule and review points after we discuss the question and available access. Recruitment, study methods, analysis depth and stakeholder needs affect the scope.

What does your next decision depend on?

Tell me what your team needs to understand. We can turn the question into a focused research scope.
